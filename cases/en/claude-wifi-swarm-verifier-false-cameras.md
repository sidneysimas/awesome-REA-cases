# 64 Claude agents on home Wi-Fi: a verifier reportedly rejects nine camera guesses

Category: Hardware and interoperability · REA use unconfirmed
Record date: 2026-10-21
Stage: Needs verification

At 21:35 Beijing time on October 21, 2026, Vexo (@Vexo200) describes 64 Claude Code agents inspecting home Wi-Fi, with a central verifier rejecting findings unsupported by captures. The author reports 41 devices and 1,206 connections traced in 11 minutes; a TV contacting ad trackers 2,940 times in a day before being disabled; a $19 plug contacting an overseas server every 90 seconds; and a printer on 2019 firmware with port 9100 open to the whole network. Without an external vantage point, the latter does not establish public-internet exposure.

The headline's nine hidden cameras are explicitly rejected in the body: guesses involving a smoke detector, baby monitor and speaker could not produce a video packet and all nine were reportedly discarded in verify.log. This is not destruction of nine cameras. Absence of a captured video packet also does not prove a device contains no camera. The author additionally claims Community Notes corrected a hotel smoke-detector story, but the corresponding note was not checked here and cannot establish whether other candidates are true or false.

No captures, verify.log, code or device models are public in the supplied evidence; keeping the password in a keychain is also an author claim. Neither the figures nor the corrections were independently checked. This is an unverified experiment in constraining AI false positives during network analysis, not an accommodation-safety guarantee or general scanning tutorial. REA involvement is unconfirmed.
