# Humans versus agents in software reverse engineering

Category: Algorithms and feature recovery · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-10-15
Stage: Analysis test

The candidate shares Doria et al.'s Humans vs. Machines: A Comparison With AI Agents in Software Reverse Engineering. Reading the arXiv abstract on October 15 established an original submission date of October 10, rather than an experiment newly completed in this window. The study has nine models perform two compact static reverse-engineering tasks previously analyzed by humans, using a common understanding score and recording IDA Pro interactions. The abstract reports statistically human-competitive understanding from the strongest agents and weaker results from weaker models. Agents tend toward broad retrieval with few function revisits, while humans more often repeatedly inspect local structure. A further 4,000 real binaries are used to analyze context saturation, emphasizing selective retrieval, navigation and persistent state. This collection checked only the abstract and did not rerun experiments. This is not an REA evaluation, and two tasks' results are not generalized to all real programs.

Original paper submitted 2026-10-10; shared this window; follow-up check only confirms research subject and abstract results.
