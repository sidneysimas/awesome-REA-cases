# Roadster video: a Claude-assisted three.js visual teardown experiment

Category: Hardware and interoperability · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-10-18
Stage: Analysis test

At 06:38 Beijing time on October 17, @Flinzu says reference frames from a Tesla Roadster demonstration were given to Claude Code to study appearance and motion without blueprints, then separate and label parts in three.js and produce a 20-second exploded-view animation. The post describes first establishing wheelbase and roofline, tying claims to frames and using deterministic frame rendering. The author says an initial box-like model looked like a toy and improved only after proportions were corrected, while six of fourteen parts stayed unknown because they were not visible.

This is an author report of image-based shape inference and visualization, not vehicle-software inspection or a physical teardown. Lift, flips, thrust and '46 million views' come from the post's narrative about the video, not this collection's verified vehicle performance, structure, manufacturability or engagement snapshot. REA is an analogy to app analysis, not a reported vehicle tool. The original video's authenticity, model code and render output were not checked, and speculation under hidden panels is not treated as mechanical fact.

At 06:00 on October 18, another author, @razeden0, reports selecting 50 HUD frames from a 105-second launch video and having Opus 5.5 produce a motion specification. They describe ffmpeg sampling every 0.5 seconds, 15 minutes of setup and a $0.94 token bill, and flag an inconsistency where the HUD shows nine feet at 16.5 seconds while the wheels are already in smoke. This is a separate image-analysis account under the same vehicle-video topic, not a new build from the earlier author. No complete frame set, specification, bill or original instrument validation is provided, and the relationship between the sampling interval and 50 selected frames is unexplained. HUD altitude, pitch and downforce are at most displayed video readings, not independent proof of real flight, fan-derived thrust or compressed-air mass. Comparing its bill with whole-car development costs is not an equivalent-task comparison and does not establish engineering efficiency. Physical inferences were not independently recalculated or verified.

20 seconds and six of fourteen parts remaining unknown are author reports; the vehicle demonstration and internal mechanics were not verified.
REA is an analogy, not a confirmed vehicle reverse-engineering tool.
