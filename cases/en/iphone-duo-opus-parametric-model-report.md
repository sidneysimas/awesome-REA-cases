# An iPhone Duo code model: reported part-by-part Opus reconstruction and constraint checks

Category: Hardware and interoperability · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-10-24
Stage: Needs verification

At 22:51:01 Beijing time on October 23, 2026, @andreysuperior reports using Opus 5.5 to build a folding 141-part model of what the post calls the new iPhone Duo. It claims 139.9k triangles, 64 design rules and zero errors, listing a frame, inner display, logic board, two batteries, hinge, cameras and flex cables through the hinge. The stated goal is a part-by-part, checkable and animated code model of a device.

The author describes making each part a separate function, listing dimensions first, then placing and checking parts individually before proceeding. Example constraints include clearance of at least 0.05 mm, no collisions and cable bends of at least 0.8 mm, followed by folding to 150 degrees on a 64-sample torque curve. The post says it transfers the oracle-checking loop from a REA software-reconstruction guide to a phone model; that does not establish actual REA analysis of a phone.

No dimension sources, teardown, scan, engineering drawing, model code, geometry artifact or constraint-test records are supplied. Correspondence to real hardware, realistic motion/torque and actual passage of 64 rules are therefore unestablished; zero errors is not manufacturability. The 141 parts, 139.9k triangles and other values remain author claims. This prior-window source is included as a concrete, unverified AI reconstruction experiment. Product naming and release status were not verified, and no model was run.
