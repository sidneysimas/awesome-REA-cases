# eMudhra weak keys: reported attribution of public-data recovery to AI and mathematical scripts

Category: Algorithms and feature recovery · Other AI-assisted work

Stage: Needs verification

Candidates from October 23, 2026 at 12:21:06 and 13:02:10 Beijing time excerpt and summarize eMudhra cofounder Kaushik Srinivasan's statements to ET. They are earlier unresolved material reviewed now, not new October 25 events. The target is recovery of certificate-authority weak keys from public data. AI's role is the participant's explanation, not an independently confirmed toolchain.

The detailed post says a script error in an additional wrap-unwrap step during April 2025 international-business key generation produced weak keys alongside intended strong keys. Srinivasan says they conformed to standards but could be reversed with modern AI tools and scripts running mathematical algorithms. Reportedly predictable secret numbers 4, 56 and 237 and questions about checks and audits are also reported claims. No keys, certificates or scripts were inspected; the cryptographic algorithm, model contribution and attack complexity are not established.

The post also relays the company's denial of an infrastructure breach, statements that affected authorities were revoked within 14 hours of discovery and issued no end-entity certificates, and a decision to leave the global SSL/TLS certificate business. Proposed safeguards include stronger key-ceremony oversight, maker-checker controls and AI strength checks. It says the cofounder considered an independent audit unnecessary because the cause was known, while considering external auditors for the process. The original ET article, official investigation, model calls and replayable mathematics were not obtained. Timing, business decisions and non-issuance remain attributed to the candidate's account. This records a concrete but disputed AI-reversing attribution, not evidence AI broke normal strong keys, breached infrastructure or actually forged certificates. No keys were accessed.
