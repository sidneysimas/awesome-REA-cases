# Cognizant knowledge layer: seven-agent recovery from 241 repositories, then service-scoped rule retrieval

Category: Applications and clients · Other AI-assisted work
Record date: 2026-10-26
Stage: Analysis test

The candidate published at 16:30 Beijing time on October 26, 2026 transcribes Garima Agrawal and other Cognizant researchers' paper, Reimagining Enterprise RAG for Multi-Agent Systems: A Knowledge Layer That Organizes Before It Retrieves. It identifies arXiv:2610.32851v1 and an original date of October 23, 2026. This is earlier research shared in the current window, not an experiment newly dated to the share.

The target is undocumented enterprise application behavior, not binary cracking. The authors report a seven-agent reverse-engineering pipeline that processed 241 repositories across 18 services into source-referenced scope definitions, journeys, business rules, acceptance criteria and gap registers. One of 14 workflows may involve 112 endpoint requirements documents. Terms such as offer and eligibility recur across services but are not interchangeable, allowing flat vector retrieval to cite the wrong service's rule.

Their knowledge layer organizes artifacts by service, domain, feature and type in a catalog, sparse metadata registry and artifact-level hypergraph. It first resolves a question to artifact identifiers, then runs hybrid dense/BM25 search only over that set. An LLM confidence gate widens to the whole corpus if scoped evidence is insufficient. Runtime context graphs preserve rule-to-repository provenance for developer review. The authors say the layer was built in under a week, admitted 18 services without schema changes, supported analysis of more than 100 production defects and attributed 35 release regression failures to rules and services. Analyzing over 100 defects must not be rewritten as automatically fixing them all.

Deployment and numbers come from the paper as transcribed in the candidate; enterprise repositories, defect tickets and independent execution records were not obtained. The candidate ends during its Evidence blocked discussion, but the earlier text identifies the method and bounded outcomes. Retrieval accuracy remains under evaluation; no comparable accuracy, widening rate or ablation data was obtained. This does not establish superiority to all RAG designs. REA use is not mentioned and no target was executed here.
