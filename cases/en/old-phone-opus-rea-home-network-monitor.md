# An old phone as a home Wi-Fi monitor: a 14-device Opus/REA report

Category: Hardware and interoperability · REA
Record date: 2026-10-21
Stage: Needs verification

At 18:45 Beijing time on October 21, 2026, a publisher reports repurposing a phone left in a drawer for two years as a home Wi-Fi monitor. Opus 5.5 reportedly makes decisions, REA analyzes devices and short reports return to the model. The goal is repeated inventory of apartment-network devices and exposed services, not merely a phone interface. The author says the first sweep found 14 devices, including two they did not recognize, followed by automatic hourly rechecks without further manual intervention.

No phone model, OS, scanning tools, permissions, device inventory, topology, external vantage point, repository or logs are provided. Unfamiliar devices do not establish an intrusion, and a service visible on a LAN is not necessarily publicly exposed. The 24/7 operation and low token use are author descriptions without duration or usage measurements. The post's broader claim about reversing anything with a chip is not adopted. This remains an unverified home-monitoring experiment; no network scanning, device control or alert-accuracy verification was performed here.
