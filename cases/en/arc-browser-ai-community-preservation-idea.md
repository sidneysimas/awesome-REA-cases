# Preserving Arc: readers propose REA and Codex-assisted community reconstruction after its sunset announcement

Category: Applications and clients · REA
Record date: 2026-10-28
Stage: Needs verification

At 01:59:12 Beijing time on October 23, 2026, éric salinas (@ericsalinas) replies to Arc's official announcement, suggesting that declining to open source the browser invites reverse engineering and calling for the REA busters. At 02:02:49, Rishi Shah (@rishhishah) similarly calls for Arc to be open sourced or for reverse-engineering Codex agents to assemble so the community can keep it alive. These proposals concern one preservation target, not multiple projects already underway.

A supplementary read of éric's parent thread identifies the context: at 00:08:24 that day Arc officially announced a December 22, 2026 sunset, saying engineering and security teams would focus on Dia. It describes migration of passwords, bookmarks, extensions, history and Spaces-related organization. The official post only establishes the product and announced date; it is not another candidate, and vendor claims that Dia is safer, faster or more stable are not treated as independent comparisons.

At 11:29:52, Debdeep Sen (@DebdeepSen7) separately claimed to have recreated Arc in one hour using REA and Claude, with open sourcing planned after some polishing. Rereading the same original post confirmed identity and a photo attachment but provided no readable feature list, repository, analysis logs or running tests. This is a new completion claim for the same target, not proof of recovering a native browser engine, synchronization service, extension compatibility or security maintenance. The poster is not assumed to share a team with the earlier proponents.

At 19:44:43 on October 28, luiz nickel (@vaporwavie), replying to @hiimmehdi, proposes studying Arc's core with REA, building Chromium in a Hetzner VM, porting patches to Arc and maintaining a personal fork. Original-post lookup confirms attribution. This is a reader's concrete build/patch-maintenance idea, not acquisition of Arc's core, a completed build or a promise of sustained security updates. It is not assumed to be Debdeep's implementation.

The early requests showed no executed research; the newer one-hour account remains an unverified author claim. No organized team or permission arrangement is supplied, and complete source recovery, sustainable security updates or participation by unrelated teams such as Beamdog cannot be inferred. REA and Codex are routes suggested by different readers, not an integrated stack already used. Status remains unverified, without claiming Arc has been open sourced, rebuilt equivalently or already shut down. This collection handled no browser data or accounts and executed no target code.
