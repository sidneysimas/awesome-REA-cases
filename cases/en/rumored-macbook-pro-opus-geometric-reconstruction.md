At 06:00:15 Beijing time on October 25, 2026, Superior (@andreysuperior) describes reconstructing a MacBook Pro assembly from rumors and leaks. Each part is code, placed and checked for clearances, clashes, cable bends and airflow paths; failures are corrected before continuing, and moving parts are animated last. The reusable prompt first separates confirmed from rumored specifications, lists dimensions and parts, and waits for human approval before coding. It borrows the oracle idea from REA software reconstruction, without establishing that REA analyzed laptop hardware.

The poster reports 126 parts, 335k triangles, 259 passing design rules, zero remaining errors and a lid opening to 112 degrees. Checks reportedly require at least 0.05mm clearance; the lid sits 0.14mm above the keys, while a minimum cable-bend radius of 0.8mm is met by a 4mm model radius. A heat pipe allegedly blocked a fan outlet by 0.60mm; moving it 1.5mm left 0.90mm clearance. The OLED touchscreen, Dynamic Island, M5 Pro/Max, six battery cells, two fans and heat pipes are specifications assumed in this posted model, not Apple product information confirmed here. An October 28 launch is also the author's prediction.

No model source, dimension references or replayable check records were obtained. The attached video was not watched and no CAD was run. Passing geometry rules establishes neither real airflow, cooling, strength, manufacturing tolerances nor correct product specifications. This illustrative model is not presented as recovery of an actual machine.
